# Work section direction — sell promotion and channel strategy

## Decision

Keep a proof section, but replace the six-item graphics gallery as the primary showcase. The user has confirmed that promotion and channel strategy are the main services to sell. The current gallery mostly demonstrates visual styles; it does not demonstrate audience positioning, promotion planning, or the quality of strategic recommendations.

The recommended section is **“A look inside my strategy work.”** It should let a visitor inspect a small sample of the thinking and deliverables they could hire Emmanuel for. Graphics can remain in a secondary design-work section when those services are being sold.

## Diagnosis of the screenshot

- Six equally weighted rectangles make the visitor work out which example is relevant.
- Abstract identity samples are evidence of visual direction; they are not evidence of promotion expertise.
- The heading describes variety rather than a buyer's problem or a specific kind of help.
- Category filters prioritize asset types, which supports a graphics-selection experience.
- The most commercially relevant information is hidden in modals: what the work is for, what is delivered, and how a person can request it.
- There is little visible connection between the samples and the main service Emmanuel now wants to sell.

These are qualitative observations, not measured conversion findings.

| Strategy sample / promotion-plan walkthrough | Thinking, clarity, practical deliverables, and an inquiry path | An honest self-initiated example, clearly labeled                           | Best option now                             |

Removing proof entirely would leave the promotion offer more abstract. Change the kind of proof instead.

## Proposed composition

### 1. A specific heading

Eyebrow: **A LOOK INSIDE MY STRATEGY WORK**

Heading: **Good content deserves a considered promotion plan.**

Supporting copy: “Here’s how I think about helping the right people discover your channel: a clearer reason to watch, content worth sharing, and a realistic way to put it out there.”

Keep the concept disclosure visible beside this introduction: **Illustrative strategy samples. Fictional channels. No campaign results implied.**

### 2. One substantial flagship sample

Example: **Afterlight — give the process a story.** A fictional art and conversation streamer. Show the brief and the reasoning, not an invented success story.

Three keyboard-accessible views:

- **The channel angle:** An invented generic message compared with a more specific content angle; explain the reason to watch and invitation to return. Clearly disclose that the messages are examples.
- **The promotion plan:** Connect a stream idea to usable excerpts and appropriate placements. Each asset needs context; outside community sharing depends on that community's rules. Do not imply automated distribution, mass posting, or paid ads are included.
- **The review rhythm:** Agree the focus, record what gets published, and review relevant native information the creator chooses to share. Mention that correlation alone does not establish campaign causation.

Visible potential deliverables: positioning notes, content angles, placement recommendations, a promotion calendar, repurposing guidance, and review priorities. Final scope is agreed with the client; these are not a universal fixed package.

### 3. Two smaller audience-specific examples

- **Gaming:** A repeatable challenge or series provides a more specific promotional story than isolated clips. Show sample content structure, not game characters or fake view counts.
- **Variety:** A recognizable point of view and recurring conversation format connect changing topics. Show sample positioning notes, not fabricated channel-performance analysis.

This demonstrates that the recommendation depends on the content instead of implying one routine fits every creator.

### 4. A direct, low-friction next step

Heading: **Tell me what you stream. And where you feel stuck.**

Ask for the channel link, where they currently promote, and the goal they want help with. CTA: **Send Emmanuel my channel.**

The prototype links to the real public email already configured on the portfolio. It opens a draft; it does not submit a lead or send mail automatically.

## Making the offer commercially stronger

Show the actual work a buyer is considering, make the scope understandable, explain what input is needed, and give them a short path to a conversation. Credibility can come from a useful sample document rather than a follower-growth promise.

Avoid invented numbers, guaranteed outcomes, client logos, false scarcity, unsupported response-time promises, or a free detailed strategy offer unless Emmanuel chooses to provide it. A project should promise agreed work and communication, not an uncontrollable audience result.

Do not turn the sample into an automated audit, software dashboard, pricing configurator, or “growth score.” This is a personal services portfolio.

## What the separate prototype is for

`public/work-section-concept.html` is a no-index design-direction preview with real tabs and email inquiry links. It does not replace the main portfolio section. It is a reviewable concept, not a claim that the public competitor research was completed.

If adopted, integrate the composition into the existing React page, replace the old asset-type filters, carry the chosen service into the inquiry, and keep the fictional-sample disclosure visible. The rest of the portfolio will also need promotion to lead the hero and service hierarchy for consistency; that is a subsequent scope decision rather than an unrelated change made here.
